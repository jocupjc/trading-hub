Tilt emergency protocol
Physical signals: Elevated HR · Jaw tension · Faster typing · Mentally justifying a bad trade · Impatience while waiting for a setup
01 · STOP
Open no new positions. Hands off the keyboard.
02 · Breathing exercise
4-7-8: inhale 4s, hold 7s, exhale 8s. 3 rounds. Activate cortisol reduction.
03 · Reality check
"Would I take this trade in a normal state?" — If no: don't trade.
04 · 15-minute break
Leave the screen. A short walk. No "just a quick look".
05 · Document
Note the tilt trigger in the journal immediately. Data for later pattern analysis.
Tendler's 5 core emotional problems
Tilt / Anger
Cause: expectation violated. Core: "This must not happen." → Revenge trading, over-sizing, moving the SL.
Fear
Cause: loss consequences overestimated. Core: "I can't handle this." → Undersizing, exiting early, hesitation.
Greed / FOMO
Cause: short-term reward craving. Core: "I'm missing out." → Over-trading, chasing, entering too early.
Confidence problems
Cause: identifying with trades. Core: "I'm a bad trader." → Paralysis, over-analysis, frequent strategy switching.
Arrogance / Winner's tilt
Cause: a winning streak creates a feeling of invincibility. Core: "I can't do anything wrong right now." → Over-sizing, rule breaks, ignoring the SL.
Tendler's 3 key principles
Emotions are symptoms, not the problem
The true cause is always a belief or expectation. Working on the emotion without working on the belief is treating symptoms only.
Variance is real — accept it structurally
Edge only shows over 100+ trades. Individual results are statistical noise. Train the brain to see losses as part of the distribution.
Mental performance is a skill, not a talent
Daily journaling is the training tool. Consistency over 90 days produces a measurable change in the emotional baseline.
Conscious Competence Ladder
Stage 1 · Unconsciously incompetent
You don't know what you don't know. Mistakes happen, but you don't recognize them as such.
Stage 2 · Consciously incompetent
You recognize your mistakes but can't prevent them yet. Journaling starts to work here.
Stage 3 · Consciously competent
You can act correctly, but it requires conscious effort. The pre-trading routine stabilizes this stage.
Stage 4 · Unconsciously competent
Correct action becomes automatic. The goal: bring the C-Game to stage 3, the A-Game to stage 4.
